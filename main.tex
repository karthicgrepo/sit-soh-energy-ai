\begin{abstract}
Accurate state-of-health (SOH) forecasting is important for battery management, yet
most machine-learning studies are evaluated on small laboratory cells with rolling
one-step-ahead prediction, where the model is continuously supplied with the latest
observed SOH. This study examines whether such benchmarks reflect the challenges of
large-format battery deployment. We analyse an 18-month dataset of 20 large-format
50\,Ah LiFePO$_4$ prismatic cells tested under three operating conditions, together
with several external battery datasets. Substantial cell-to-cell variation is
observed even among nominally identical cells, while a comparison of six
deep-learning models shows that conventional one-step forecasting is close to
saturation: a simple linear autoregressive model performs as well as or better than
the deep models, and cell identity explains substantially more prediction-error
variance than model choice.

These findings motivate a deployment-oriented framework that targets capabilities
beyond one-step accuracy. The proposed hybrid model combines LFP pretraining, an
early-life fingerprint encoder over the first 30 cycles, target-cell fine-tuning, and
uncertainty estimation. Without target-cell fine-tuning, fingerprint-conditioned
transfer reduces RMSE by 23\% relative to training from scratch, and subsequent
fine-tuning increases the average reduction to 51\% over the same scratch baseline.
The framework is further evaluated for uncertainty estimation, early identification
of fast-degrading cells, cross-dataset transfer, and remaining-useful-life
forecasting. Early-life voltage-curve features flag cells at risk of premature
degradation with an AUC of 0.78, and the RUL model reaches a median error of 49
cycles at the midlife forecasting point. The proposed hybrid is not intended to win
the already-saturated one-step benchmark; its value is transfer to a new cell,
uncertainty estimation, early screening, and long-term forecasting. Overall, the main
challenge in large-format battery prognostics is not further reducing already-small
one-step errors, but developing models that adapt across cells, quantify uncertainty,
identify abnormal degradation early, and provide reliable long-term forecasts.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data and Code Availability Statement}
The SIT large-format LiFePO$_4$ dataset analysed in this study is openly
available at \url{https://doi.org/10.25447/sit.32101523}~\cite{ponnambalam2026sitdata}.
The MIT/Severson fast-charging dataset is openly available from Severson et
al.~\cite{severson2019data}, and the NASA dataset from the NASA Prognostics
Center of Excellence~\cite{saha2007battery}.
This study is designed to be fully reproducible. All code, the complete
experimental pipeline, and the selected pretrained checkpoint that generate every
table and figure in this paper are available at
\url{https://github.com/karthicgrepo/battery-soh-prediction}.
Every experiment runs deterministically, using TensorFlow op-determinism with full
float32 precision and complete seeding of the Python, NumPy, and TensorFlow random
number generators, so each reported value reproduces bit-for-bit on the same
hardware and software stack.
